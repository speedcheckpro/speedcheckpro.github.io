How to Lower Ping in Games: 10 Proven Fixes

SpeedCheck Pro Guide • Updated November 2026 • 6 min read

Nothing ruins a good match like high ping. You press a button, your character reacts a beat too late, and by the time your shot registers, the round is already lost. Ping is the time it takes for a tiny packet of data to travel from your device to the game server and back, measured in milliseconds. The lower the number, the faster the game responds to you. Under 30 ms feels instant, 30 to 60 ms is perfectly playable, and anything above 100 ms starts to feel sluggish. The good news is that most high ping has a fixable cause, and many of the fixes cost nothing.

Use a Wired Connection First

The single biggest improvement most players can make is switching from Wi-Fi to an Ethernet cable. Wi-Fi adds delay because your signal fights through walls, furniture, and interference from neighbouring networks. A cable removes all of that. Even a long cable run across the room is worth it for competitive play. If running a cable is impossible, move closer to the router or use a powerline adapter as a middle ground. Our guide to Wi-Fi vs Ethernet explains the difference in detail.

Pick the Closest Game Server

Distance is the one factor you cannot cheat. Data travels fast, but it still takes time to cross continents. A server in your own country will almost always give you lower ping than one on the other side of the world. Most games let you choose a region in the settings menu. Pick the region closest to where you live, and avoid the "auto" setting if it keeps sending you to a faraway server. If your game shows ping next to each region, test them all and stick with the lowest.

Close Background Apps and Downloads

Your game shares your connection with everything else on your device. Cloud backups, game launchers downloading updates, streaming music, and browser tabs with video can all steal bandwidth and add delay. Before an important match, close download managers, pause cloud sync, and shut down apps you do not need. The same goes for other people in your house: a family member starting a 4K stream mid-match will spike your ping. A quick word before you play can save a ranked game.

Restart Your Router and Modem

Routers and modems run for weeks without a break, and over time their memory fills up and their performance drops. A simple restart clears the backlog and forces a fresh connection to your provider. Unplug both devices, wait thirty seconds, plug the modem back in first, and wait for it to fully connect before powering up the router. Do this once a week, and always before a long gaming session if your ping has been creeping up.

Tip: Test your ping at the same time of day you usually play. Evening congestion can add 20 to 30 ms compared with the morning, so a test at noon will not tell you how your connection behaves at 8 p.m.

Check for Congestion and Interference

If your ping is fine in the morning but terrible at night, congestion is the likely culprit. Your neighbours are all online at the same time, and your provider's local network is under strain. A wired connection helps, and so does asking your provider whether your area is oversubscribed. On Wi-Fi, interference from nearby networks is another common cause. Switching your router to the 5 GHz band, or to a less crowded channel, can cut several milliseconds. Our guide on how to improve your Wi-Fi signal walks through the settings.

Update Your Network Drivers and Firmware

Outdated software is a quiet cause of high ping. Network drivers on your PC, the firmware on your router, and the game itself all get updates that fix bugs and improve performance. Check your router's admin page for a firmware update every few months, and keep your PC's network drivers current. It takes five minutes and it removes one more variable from the troubleshooting list.

When to Call Your Internet Provider

If you have tried everything and your ping is still high, the problem may be on your provider's side. Run a speed test and note your ping over several days, at different times. If your ping to a nearby server is consistently above 80 ms while your download speed looks normal, that points to routing or congestion on their network. Share your test results when you call. A provider can sometimes move you to a better route, or admit that your area needs an upgrade. If nothing changes, it may be time to compare other providers.

Lower ping is mostly about removing obstacles between you and the server: a cable instead of Wi-Fi, the nearest server, a quiet network, and up-to-date equipment. Work through the fixes one at a time, test your ping after each change, and keep the ones that help. Most players can cut 20 to 50 ms without spending anything.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a good ping for gaming?

Under 30 ms is excellent and feels instant. Between 30 and 60 ms is fine for almost every game, including competitive shooters. From 60 to 100 ms you may notice a slight delay, and above 100 ms the game starts to feel sluggish and unresponsive.

Does a faster internet plan lower ping?

Not directly. Ping measures delay, not bandwidth, so upgrading from 100 Mbps to 500 Mbps will not change your ping if the route to the server stays the same. A faster plan only helps ping when your current connection is so congested that packets are queuing up, which is rare.

Can a VPN lower ping?

Almost never. A VPN adds an extra hop between you and the game server, which usually raises ping by 10 to 30 ms. The only exception is when your provider routes your traffic badly and the VPN happens to take a shorter path, but that is uncommon and hard to predict.
