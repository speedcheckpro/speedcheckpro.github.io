How Much Speed Do I Need?

SpeedCheck Pro Guide • Updated November 2026 • 4 min read

Picking an internet plan should be simple, but the numbers make it confusing. Providers advertise speeds of 100, 500 or even 1,000 Mbps, and it is hard to know which one your home actually needs. The honest answer is that most households need far less speed than they are sold, and paying for more than you use does not make your connection feel any faster.

The right speed for you depends on three things: how many people use the internet at the same time, what they do online, and how many connected devices your home has. A single person who mainly browses the web and watches the occasional video has very different needs from a family of five where everyone streams, plays games and works from home at the same time.

What Different Activities Actually Need

Every online activity uses a certain amount of speed, measured in megabits per second (Mbps). The table below shows the rough download speed each common activity needs to run smoothly on its own. These are minimums for one person doing one thing at a time.

ActivityDownload speed needed
Browsing and email1 to 5 Mbps
Music streaming1 to 5 Mbps
Video call3 to 8 Mbps
HD video (1080p)5 to 8 Mbps
4K video streaming25 Mbps
Online gaming5 to 25 Mbps
Large downloads and cloud backupThe faster, the better

Gaming needs surprisingly little raw speed. A smooth game depends far more on low ping and a stable connection than on a huge download number, so if you play online regularly, read our guide to internet speed for gaming before choosing a plan for its download figure alone.

Add Up Your Busiest Moment

To work out your number, think about the busiest time of day in your home, which is usually the evening. Add together the speeds for everything that runs at the same time. For example, two people watching 4K video need about 50 Mbps, a video call adds around 8 Mbps, a gamer adds up to 25 Mbps, and general browsing adds a few more. That household needs roughly 90 Mbps at peak time.

A good rule is to take your busiest-moment total and add 25 to 50 percent on top. That extra room covers background updates, smart home devices and the occasional large download, so one person's software update does not freeze everyone else's video. Rounding the example above up to 100 Mbps gives a comfortable plan for that family.

Speed by Household Size

If you do not want to add up activities, household size is a useful shortcut:

  • 1 to 2 people, light use: 25 to 50 Mbps is usually enough for browsing, email, music and HD video.
  • 3 to 4 people, regular streaming: 100 to 200 Mbps covers several HD or 4K streams at once.
  • 5 or more people, or heavy use: 200 to 500 Mbps gives everyone room to stream, game and work at the same time.
  • Gigabit (1,000 Mbps): only worth it for very large households, creators who upload huge files, or homes with many 4K streams running at once.

If you work from home, upload speed matters as much as download speed. Video calls, cloud drives and sending large files all depend on upload, so check the upload figure in any plan you consider. Our guide to internet speed for working from home explains what remote workers should look for.

When a Faster Plan Will Not Help

A faster plan cannot fix a weak Wi-Fi signal, an old router, or congestion on your provider's network. If a speed test shows you are getting the speed you pay for but pages still load slowly, the problem is somewhere else, and paying for a bigger plan will only cost more for the same experience.

Quick test: run a speed test next to your router, then run it again in the room where the problem happens. If the numbers are very different, your Wi-Fi is the problem, not your plan, and a faster plan will not fix it.

If your test results are fine near the router but poor elsewhere, the fix is usually better Wi-Fi coverage, not a bigger plan. Our guide to improving your Wi-Fi signal walks through router placement and other free fixes.

How to Check What You Actually Get

Run a speed test at your busiest time of day, on the device you use most, while other people in the house are online. Compare the result with the speed in your plan. If you consistently get less than 80 percent of what you pay for, contact your provider, because something is wrong on their side.

If you get what you pay for but it still feels slow, use the table above to see whether your plan is simply too small for your household. And if you are still unsure what the numbers mean, our guide to what a good internet speed is explains the benchmarks in plain English. Pick the smallest plan that covers your busiest moment with room to spare, and you will get the best value for your money.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is 100 Mbps fast enough for a family of four?

Yes, for most families. 100 Mbps comfortably covers several HD streams, video calls and browsing at the same time. You only need more if several people stream 4K video at once or regularly upload very large files.

Do I need gigabit internet?

Very few homes do. Gigabit speeds help when many people stream in 4K at once or you move huge files every day. For ordinary browsing, streaming and gaming, 100 to 300 Mbps feels the same as gigabit.

Why does my internet feel slow even though I have a fast plan?

The usual causes are weak Wi-Fi coverage, an old router, or peak-hour congestion on your provider's network. Test next to the router first: if the speed is fine there, the problem is your Wi-Fi, not your plan.
