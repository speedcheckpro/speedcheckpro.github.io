Internet Speed for Streaming 4K: What You Need

SpeedCheck Pro Guide • Updated November 2026 • 5 min read

4K video looks stunning on a big screen, but only when your internet can keep up. A 4K stream carries four times the detail of standard high definition, and it needs a steady flow of data to play without pausing to buffer or dropping back to a blurry picture. The good news is that you do not need a gigabit plan for 4K. Most homes can stream it smoothly on a mid-range plan, as long as the connection is stable and the Wi-Fi reaches the TV properly.

How Much Speed Does 4K Streaming Need?

Streaming services publish their own recommendations, and they agree closely. Netflix suggests 15 Mbps for a 4K stream, Amazon Prime Video suggests the same, and YouTube recommends around 20 Mbps for 4K playback. In practice, you should aim for a little more than the minimum, because your connection speed varies from moment to moment. A steady 25 Mbps for each 4K stream is a safe target, and it gives the stream room to handle busy scenes with lots of movement. Lower qualities need far less: standard definition plays happily on 3 Mbps, and Full HD needs around 5 to 8 Mbps. The jump to 4K is the big one, because the picture carries so much more information.

Data use is worth keeping in mind too. A single two-hour 4K film uses roughly 14 GB, so a household that watches 4K every evening can use several hundred gigabytes a month on streaming alone. If your plan has a data cap, check it before switching everything to 4K. For general household planning, see our how much speed do I need guide.

What If Several People Stream at Once?

Speed needs add up. Two 4K streams at the same time need around 50 Mbps just for video, before you count phones, video calls, or a game downloading in the background. A family with two televisions and a few tablets should look for a plan of 100 Mbps or more, which leaves comfortable room for everyone. This is where many people discover their plan is too small: a single 4K stream works fine, but the second one starts buffering. Our what is a good internet speed guide explains how to size a plan for a whole household.

Why Does 4K Keep Buffering?

Buffering rarely means your plan is too slow. More often, the problem is between the router and the TV. A television at the far end of the house, behind thick walls, may only receive a fraction of your plan speed over Wi-Fi. Peak evening hours can also slow things down, when the whole neighbourhood is streaming at once. Sometimes the streaming service itself drops the quality to match a shaky connection, so the picture goes soft even though the film keeps playing. If buffering happens on one TV but not on a phone in the same room, the TV's Wi-Fi is the weak link. If it happens everywhere at the same time, the line itself may be congested. Our guide to how to improve Wi-Fi signal walks through the fixes.

Wi-Fi or a Cable for the TV?

For a main living-room television, an ethernet cable is the most reliable option. It delivers the full speed of your plan with no interference, which is exactly what a 4K stream needs. If running a cable is not practical, use the 5 GHz Wi-Fi band and keep the router as close to the TV as you can, ideally in the same room. A mesh Wi-Fi system can help in larger homes by placing a second unit near the TV. Avoid relying on a weak 2.4 GHz signal from two rooms away, since that is the most common cause of 4K buffering. The type of connection you have matters too: fibre handles 4K easily, while older lines may struggle at peak times, as explained in our fibre vs cable vs DSL guide.

Tip: If a film keeps dropping out of 4K, set the streaming app to high quality manually instead of auto. Auto mode drops the quality at the first sign of a slowdown, while a fixed setting will buffer briefly and then stay sharp.

For most homes, a 100 Mbps plan with a solid Wi-Fi signal at the TV is enough for smooth 4K, even with a second stream running. Before upgrading your plan, test your actual speed at the television itself: run a test on a phone or laptop in the same spot and see what it really receives. If that number is well below your plan speed, fix the Wi-Fi first, because a faster plan will not fix a weak signal.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is 25 Mbps enough for 4K streaming?

Yes, for one 4K stream. Netflix recommends 15 Mbps and YouTube around 20 Mbps, so 25 Mbps gives you a comfortable margin.

Why does my 4K stream keep dropping to HD?

Streaming apps lower the quality automatically when the connection wavers. A weak Wi-Fi signal at the TV is the most common cause.

Does 4K streaming use a lot of data?

Yes, about 7 GB per hour. A two-hour 4K film uses roughly 14 GB, so check your data cap if your plan has one.
