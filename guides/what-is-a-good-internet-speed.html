What Is a Good Internet Speed?

SpeedCheck Pro Guide • Updated November 2026 • 5 min read

A good internet speed is one that lets everyone in your home do what they need to do online at the same time, without buffering, freezing, or long waits. There is no single magic number, because the right speed depends on how many people use your connection, how many devices are connected, and what those devices are doing. A household with one person checking email needs far less than a family of five streaming films, playing games, and joining video calls at once. This guide explains the numbers in plain language, so you can work out whether your current plan is enough, or whether you are paying for speed you will never use.

What internet speed actually means

Internet speed is measured in megabits per second, usually written as Mbps. It describes how much data your connection can move in one second. A higher number means more data per second, which means faster downloads, smoother video, and quicker page loads. Speed is usually split into two directions. Download speed is how fast data comes into your home, and it matters most for streaming, browsing, and downloading files. Upload speed is how fast data leaves your home, and it matters for video calls, sending files, and live streaming. Most home plans advertise the download speed in big print and the upload speed in small print, so it helps to check both. You can learn more in our guide to download vs upload speed.

How much speed does your household need?

As a starting point, match your plan to the size of your household and how heavily you use the internet. The table below gives practical ranges that work for most homes.

HouseholdRecommended download speed
One person, light use25 Mbps
One or two people, streaming and browsing50 to 100 Mbps
Three or four people, several streams and calls100 to 200 Mbps
Five or more people, gaming and 4K streaming200 to 500 Mbps

These are practical starting points, not hard rules. If your household watches a lot of 4K video or plays online games while someone else is on a video call, aim for the higher end of your band. If you mostly browse and watch standard-definition video, the lower end is fine. Our guide to how much speed you need walks through the calculation for your own home in more detail.

What different activities need

Most everyday activities need far less speed than people expect. Here are typical download speeds for one stream or session at a time:

  • Browsing the web and email: 1 to 5 Mbps
  • Music streaming: 1 to 5 Mbps
  • High-definition video: 5 to 8 Mbps
  • 4K ultra-high-definition video: about 25 Mbps
  • Video calls: 3 to 8 Mbps
  • Online gaming: 5 to 25 Mbps, with low ping mattering more than raw speed
  • Downloading large files: the faster the better, but any plan above 50 Mbps feels quick

Notice how small most of these numbers are. A single 4K stream needs about 25 Mbps, so a 100 Mbps plan can comfortably run three 4K streams at once, with room left over. Problems start when several demanding activities happen at the same time, or when your Wi-Fi signal is weak. That is why a household can feel slow even on a fast plan, and why it is worth checking the numbers before you pay for an upgrade.

Download speed is not the whole story

Two other numbers affect how your connection feels. Ping, measured in milliseconds, is the delay before data starts moving. Low ping makes video calls and games feel instant, while high ping makes them feel sluggish even on a fast plan. Upload speed matters too: if you work from home and spend your day on video calls, a plan with a slow upload will show it. For a full explanation, see what ping is and why it matters. A plan that is strong on all three, download, upload, and ping, will feel fast in everyday use.

How to check whether your speed is good enough

The simplest test is to run a speed test on the device and in the room where you actually use the internet, at the time of day when it feels slowest. Compare the result with the plan you pay for. If the result is close to your plan speed but the internet still feels slow, the problem is probably your Wi-Fi coverage or an overloaded router rather than the plan itself. If the result is far below your plan speed on a wired connection, contact your provider. Run the test a few times on different days before you decide to upgrade, because speeds can dip during busy evening hours when many neighbours are online.

Tip: Test your speed in the room where you use the internet most, not next to the router. A result of 100 Mbps beside the router and 15 Mbps in the bedroom tells you the Wi-Fi is the problem, not the plan.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a good internet speed for a family of four?

For most families of four, 100 to 200 Mbps download is a comfortable range. It covers several video streams, video calls, and browsing at the same time. If the family also plays online games or watches a lot of 4K video, aim toward 200 Mbps or above.

Is 100 Mbps fast enough for streaming?

Yes. A single 4K stream needs about 25 Mbps, and a high-definition stream needs 5 to 8 Mbps, so 100 Mbps can handle several streams at once. If streams buffer on a 100 Mbps plan, the cause is usually weak Wi-Fi rather than the plan itself.

Why is my internet slow if I pay for a fast plan?

The most common causes are a weak Wi-Fi signal in the room you use, an old router, too many devices sharing the connection, or congestion at busy times. Test with a wired connection first: if the wired speed matches your plan, the plan is fine and the Wi-Fi needs attention.
