What Is Mbps and Gbps?

SpeedCheck Pro Guide • Updated November 2026 • 4 min read

Every internet plan is sold with a number followed by Mbps or Gbps, and every speed test reports its result in the same units. If you have ever wondered what those letters actually mean, or why a 100 Mbps plan does not download a 100 MB file in one second, this guide explains it in plain English.

Once you understand the two units, you can read any plan, compare providers fairly and work out how long a download will really take.

What Is a Bit?

A bit is the smallest piece of digital information: a single 1 or 0. Everything you do online — a photo, a song, a video — is made of millions or billions of bits. Internet speed measures how many bits travel through your connection each second, which is why speed is a rate, like kilometres per hour, not a total amount.

Eight bits make one byte. That single fact explains most of the confusion about internet speeds, as you will see below.

What Is Mbps?

Mbps stands for megabits per second. One megabit is one million bits, so a 100 Mbps connection can move 100 million bits every second. Mbps is the unit used for almost all home internet plans and speed test results, from a basic 25 Mbps plan to a fast 500 Mbps plan.

To get a feel for the numbers, a 25 Mbps connection can stream one HD video comfortably, 100 Mbps handles several streams and video calls at once, and 300 Mbps covers a busy family home. Our guide to what a good internet speed is puts these numbers into everyday context.

What Is Gbps?

Gbps stands for gigabits per second. One gigabit is one thousand megabits, so 1 Gbps equals 1,000 Mbps. Gigabit plans are the fastest home connections widely sold today, and they are usually sold as "gigabit" or "1 Gig" rather than as 1,000 Mbps.

A gigabit connection can download a large game in minutes and supports many 4K streams at once. For most households it is more speed than they can use, because the bottleneck is usually the Wi-Fi or the device, not the connection itself.

Mbps vs MBps: The Capital Letter That Matters

This is the detail that confuses almost everyone. Mbps, with a small b, means megabits per second and measures internet speed. MBps, with a capital B, means megabytes per second and measures file size and download progress. Because one byte is eight bits, the two numbers differ by a factor of eight.

That is why a 100 MB file takes about eight seconds on a 100 Mbps connection, not one second: the file is 100 megabytes, the connection moves 100 megabits per second, and it takes eight megabits to make one megabyte. What the Numbers Mean in Real Life

Speed numbers only matter in terms of what you can do with them. A 4K video stream needs about 25 Mbps, a video call needs 3 to 8 Mbps, and online gaming needs 5 to 25 Mbps but cares far more about low ping than raw speed. Adding up the activities your household runs at the same time tells you the speed you actually need, and our guide to how much speed you need walks through that calculation.

Remember that the advertised speed is a maximum, not a promise. Your real speed depends on your Wi-Fi, your device, the time of day and the server at the other end. That is why it is worth testing your connection properly: our guide to testing your internet speed accurately shows how to get a result you can trust.

Quick maths: to turn a plan speed in Mbps into the fastest download rate you will see in MBps, divide by eight. A 200 Mbps plan downloads at up to about 25 MBps, so a 1 GB file takes roughly 40 seconds at full speed.

Why Providers Advertise in Mbps

Providers use megabits because the numbers look bigger and because network equipment is rated in bits per second: it is the industry standard, not a trick aimed at you. Once you know the divide-by-eight rule, you can translate any advertised speed into real download times in seconds, and compare plans on equal terms.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does Mbps stand for?

Mbps stands for megabits per second. It measures how many millions of bits of data your connection can move each second. It is the standard unit for internet plan speeds and speed test results.

Is 1 Gbps the same as 1,000 Mbps?

Yes. One gigabit per second equals 1,000 megabits per second. Providers usually advertise gigabit plans as '1 Gig' or 'gigabit' instead of writing 1,000 Mbps.

Why is my download speed in MBps lower than my plan's Mbps?

Because they are different units: your plan is measured in megabits (small b) and your download progress is shown in megabytes (capital B). One byte is eight bits, so divide the Mbps figure by eight to get the fastest download rate in MBps.
