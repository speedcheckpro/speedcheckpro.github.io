Internet Speed for Video Calls: Zoom, Teams and Meet

SpeedCheck Pro Guide • Updated November 2026 • 5 min read

Video calls have become a normal part of work, study, and keeping in touch with family. Yet few things are as frustrating as a call that freezes mid-sentence, drops your audio, or turns your face into a blur. The good news is that video calls need far less speed than most people think. A clear, stable call depends more on your upload speed and connection quality than on a huge download figure, and most problems can be fixed without upgrading your plan.

How Much Speed Do Video Calls Need?

Zoom, Microsoft Teams, and Google Meet all publish similar requirements. A one-to-one video call in standard quality needs about 1.5 Mbps down and 1.5 Mbps up. High-definition video raises that to around 3 Mbps in each direction, and a group call with many participants can need 3 to 8 Mbps down. Screen sharing adds a little more, usually 1 to 2 Mbps of upload. These numbers are small compared with streaming or gaming, which is why even a basic broadband plan can handle a call. The full requirements look like this:

Call typeDownloadUpload
One-to-one, standard quality1.5 Mbps1.5 Mbps
One-to-one, HD video3 Mbps3 Mbps
Group call, HD video3 to 8 Mbps3 Mbps
Screen sharing plus video3 Mbps4 to 5 Mbps

Upload speed is the number most people overlook, and it is the one that matters most for how others see and hear you. Many older broadband plans offer fast downloads but only 5 to 10 Mbps of upload, which is still enough for one or two calls, but can struggle if two people are on calls at once. Why Do Calls Freeze and Break Up?

When a call freezes, the cause is usually stability, not speed. High ping makes conversation feel delayed, so people start talking over each other. Jitter, the variation in delay, makes voices sound choppy or robotic. Packet loss, where pieces of data never arrive, causes frozen faces and missing words. All three are common on weak Wi-Fi, especially at the far end of the house, and they get worse when the connection is busy with downloads, backups, or someone streaming video. Our guide to jitter and packet loss explains these three culprits in detail.

Simple Fixes for Clearer Calls

Start with your connection. If you can, plug your laptop into the router with an ethernet cable for important calls; it is the single biggest improvement you can make. If you must use Wi-Fi, sit closer to the router and use the 5 GHz band, which is usually less crowded. Close cloud backups, large downloads, and streaming apps before the call starts, and ask others in the house to pause heavy use during an important meeting. Turning off your own video for a moment can rescue the audio when the connection struggles, and switching your camera to standard quality instead of HD halves the bandwidth your call needs. Our how to improve Wi-Fi signal guide covers the wireless fixes step by step.

Two People on Calls at the Same Time

Working from home often means two people on video calls at once, sometimes with children streaming lessons too. Two HD calls need roughly 6 Mbps of upload between them, plus a little headroom, so a plan with at least 10 Mbps of upload is a sensible minimum for a two-person household. If your upload speed is only 5 Mbps, calls will compete with each other and both will suffer. In that case, the fix is either a plan with faster upload, usually a fibre plan, or staggering calls when possible. Our internet speed for working from home guide covers the whole home-office setup.

Tip: Test your connection a few minutes before an important call, not during it. A quick test shows whether your upload speed and ping are healthy, and gives you time to switch to a cable or move closer to the router.

Video calls are one of the lightest uses of a modern connection, and most call problems come from a weak signal or a busy line rather than a slow plan. Check your upload speed, keep your connection steady, and use a cable when the call matters. Do that, and your next meeting should be the kind where you forget the technology is there at all.

Frequently Asked Questions

What upload speed do I need for Zoom?

Zoom needs about 1.5 Mbps of upload for standard video and 3 Mbps for HD video. Two people on HD calls at once need around 6 Mbps of upload.

Why does my video freeze but my audio keeps working?

Video uses far more data than audio, so when the connection struggles, the app protects the audio first and freezes the picture. Switching to standard quality video usually fixes it.

Is Wi-Fi good enough for video calls?

Yes, if the signal is strong. Sit close to the router and use the 5 GHz band. For important calls, an ethernet cable is still the most reliable option.
