Why Is My Internet Slow at Night?

SpeedCheck Pro Guide • Updated November 2026 • 4 min read

If your internet works fine during the day but crawls every evening, you are not imagining it. Night-time slowdowns are one of the most common complaints about home broadband, and they usually have a clear cause. The evening is when the most people are online at once, and that shared demand can expose weak points in your connection.

The good news is that most evening slowdowns can be diagnosed in a few minutes and several of them can be fixed for free. This guide explains the main reasons your internet slows down at night and what you can do about each one.

Peak-Hour Network Congestion

The biggest cause is simple congestion. In the evening, whole neighbourhoods come home, switch on their TVs, start streaming and join video calls. If your provider's local network is shared between many homes, as cable and some fibre networks are, all that demand competes for the same capacity, and everyone slows down a little.

This is the same reason roads jam at rush hour. The network is built for average demand, not for every home streaming in 4K at 8 pm. Providers sometimes call this contention. If your speed is fine at 3 pm but drops by half at 8 pm, congestion is the most likely explanation. A speed test run at different times of day will show the pattern clearly.

Your Neighbours' Wi-Fi Is Interfering

In flats and terraced houses, dozens of Wi-Fi networks sit close together. In the evening they are all active, and their signals overlap and interfere with yours, especially on the crowded 2.4 GHz band. Interference does not reduce the speed coming into your home, but it slows the wireless link between your router and your devices.

This is why a wired connection often feels fine while Wi-Fi feels slow in the evening. If your home is surrounded by other networks, switching to the 5 GHz band or changing your Wi-Fi channel can make a real difference. Our guide to improving your Wi-Fi signal explains how to pick a quieter channel.

Automatic Updates and Backups

Evenings are when devices do their housekeeping. Phones, laptops, games consoles and smart TVs often download updates, back up photos to the cloud and sync files in the background, and many of these tasks are scheduled for the evening or overnight. One large game update can use your entire connection for an hour.

Cloud backups are a common hidden culprit because they use upload speed, and most home plans have far less upload than download. A phone backing up a week's photos can make video calls stutter for everyone else. Check your devices for backup and update settings, and schedule large updates for the middle of the night.

Streaming Services and Their Own Load

Sometimes the slowdown is not your connection at all. Streaming services are busiest in the evening too, and their servers can struggle at peak times. If only one app buffers while everything else works, the problem is probably on the service's side, not yours. Try a different streaming service or a lower video quality to confirm.

Video quality settings matter more than people think. A 4K stream uses about five times the data of an HD stream, so two 4K streams running at once can saturate a 50 Mbps plan by themselves. If your household streams a lot in the evening, read our guide to internet speed for streaming 4K to see how much bandwidth video really needs.

What You Can Do About It

  • Test at different times: run a speed test in the afternoon and again in the evening. A big gap points to congestion.
  • Use a cable: plug your main device into the router with an ethernet cable for the evening. If it speeds up, your Wi-Fi is the weak link.
  • Change your Wi-Fi channel: use your router's settings or a Wi-Fi analyser app to find a less crowded channel.
  • Schedule updates: set phones, consoles and computers to update overnight instead of in the evening.
  • Lower video quality: switch 4K streams to HD during peak hours if several people are watching at once.
  • Talk to your provider: if speeds fall well below your plan every evening, ask about congestion in your area or a plan with a higher speed.
Quick check: if your internet is slow every night at the same time but fine at midday, it is almost certainly congestion or interference, not a broken router. Do not buy new equipment until you have tested at different times of day.

If the slowdown happens at all hours, the cause is more likely to be a faulty router, a damaged cable or a line problem. Our guide to fixing slow internet walks through those checks step by step.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is my internet slower at night than during the day?

Evenings are peak hours: more people in your area are streaming, gaming and video calling at the same time, so shared networks get congested. Wi-Fi interference from neighbours' networks also peaks in the evening.

Can my provider slow my internet down at night?

Some providers manage traffic at peak times, which can slow certain activities such as video streaming. However, most evening slowdowns come from ordinary congestion rather than deliberate throttling. A speed test at different times will show whether the drop follows a daily pattern.

Will a faster plan fix slow evenings?

It can help if congestion is the cause, because a bigger plan gives you a larger share of the shared capacity. But if the problem is Wi-Fi interference inside your home, a faster plan will not help — improving your Wi-Fi will.
