What is ping?

Ping, also called latency, is the time it takes for a tiny packet of data to travel from your device to a server and back again. It is measured in milliseconds (ms). Unlike download speed, which is about how much data you can move, ping is about how quickly your connection responds.

Low ping matters most when timing counts: online gaming, video calls and live streaming. A connection can have fast download speeds and still feel laggy if its ping is high or unstable.

What is jitter?

Jitter is how much your ping varies from one moment to the next. It is calculated from the differences between consecutive ping samples. Low jitter means a steady connection; high jitter means your latency jumps around, which causes stutter in voice calls, rubber-banding in games and buffering in live streams.

How to lower your ping

  • Use a wired Ethernet connection instead of Wi-Fi — it is almost always faster and steadier.
  • Move closer to your router, or switch to the 5 GHz Wi-Fi band if you are near it.
  • Close downloads, cloud backups and streaming on other devices while you play or call.
  • Restart your router if ping has suddenly got worse — a fresh connection often helps.
  • Connect to game or call servers in your own region when you get the choice.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a good jitter value?

Under 10 ms is excellent and under 30 ms is fine for most uses. Jitter above 30 ms can cause noticeable stutter in voice calls and games, even when your average ping looks good.

Why does my ping change between tests?

Ping varies with network congestion, the route your data takes, Wi-Fi interference and what else is using your connection. That is why this test takes 10 samples and reports the minimum, average and maximum instead of a single number.

Does this test use much data?

No. Each ping sample is a tiny zero-byte request, so the whole test uses only a few kilobytes. You can run it as often as you like, even on mobile data.
