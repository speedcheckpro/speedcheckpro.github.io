What this test measures

This speed test runs entirely in your browser and measures your connection against public test servers. It works in three phases:

  1. Ping (latency): how long a tiny request takes to reach the server and come back, measured in milliseconds. Lower is better, and it matters most for gaming and video calls.
  2. Download speed: how fast your connection can pull data down, measured in megabits per second (Mbps). This decides how quickly pages load, videos stream and files download.
  3. Upload speed: how fast your connection can send data up, also in Mbps. This matters for video calls, live streaming and backing files up to the cloud.

Every number you see is a real measurement of your connection at the moment of the test. Nothing is estimated, simulated or filled in. If the test servers cannot be reached, the test stops and tells you so instead of showing a made-up result.

How to get an accurate result

  • Close streaming apps, downloads and cloud backups while the test runs — they share your connection and will lower the result.
  • Use a wired (Ethernet) connection if you want to test your internet plan itself. On Wi-Fi, stand close to the router.
  • Run the test two or three times and compare. A single test can be affected by a busy moment on your network.
  • Test at different times of day. Many connections are slower in the evening, when the whole neighbourhood is online.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is this speed test free?

Yes. The test is completely free, with no sign-up, no app to install and no limit on how many times you run it.

Why is my speed lower than my plan promises?

Your plan speed is the maximum your provider offers, not a guarantee. Wi-Fi distance, an old router, other people using the connection and evening congestion can all lower the measured result. Try a wired connection and test again.

Does the test store my results?

No. The test runs in your browser and the results are shown only to you. Nothing is uploaded or saved on our servers.
