How the recommendation is worked out

Each activity has a typical speed requirement, based on what streaming services, video-call apps and game platforms publish. The checker adds up everything you ticked, then adds 5 Mbps download and 1 Mbps upload for each extra person using the connection at the same time, to cover their general browsing. Finally it rounds up to the nearest common plan tier — 10, 25, 50, 100, 200, 300, 500 or 1000 Mbps — so your connection has room to breathe when several things happen at once.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is 100 Mbps enough for a family?

For most families, yes. 100 Mbps comfortably covers several HD streams, video calls and gaming at the same time. You only need more if you stream in 4K on several screens at once, broadcast live, or move very large files to the cloud regularly.

Does upload speed matter?

Yes, if you make video calls, live stream, or back files up to the cloud — all of those send a lot of data. For browsing and watching video only, upload speed barely matters, which is why many plans offer far less upload than download.

Should I buy the fastest plan available?

Not necessarily. Speed above what your household uses does not make pages load or videos play any better. Use this checker to find your real need, then pick the cheapest plan that meets it with a little headroom.
